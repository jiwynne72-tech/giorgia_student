Lesson 6: Extensive Course Recap & Video Lab 🩺🎨

A complete review of everything we have covered across Lessons 1–5: grammar, vocabulary, movement verbs, quantifiers, narrative tenses, and a medical drama listening challenge.

1. Core Vocabulary & Common Pitfalls (Lessons 1–5)

Survive (verb) To continue living or existing after danger or hardship ("Not many patients survive rare diseases without a specialist.").
Fast vs. Quickly Fast is both an adjective and an adverb (a fast car / drive fast). Never say "fastly". Quickly is an adverb (finish quickly).
Lying vs. Laying Use lie / lying when resting horizontally without an object ("She was lying on the couch"). Lay requires an object ("Lay the book on the table").
Uncountable Nouns & 'The Louvre' Words like advice, homework, information, and furniture have no plural -s. In English, museums take the (the Louvre), and a portrait artist paints portraits.
Versatile 'Get' Used for become (get soaked, get tired), receive/obtain (get feedback), and arrive (get home).
Modals of Obligation & Prohibition Mustn't = strictly forbidden. Don't have to = not necessary (optional).

2. Adjectives vs. Adverbs & -ED / -ING Participles

Quick Grammar Check:
  • Good vs. Well: Good is an adjective describing nouns (a good diagnosis). Well is an adverb describing verbs/actions (she sings well, the therapy went well) or means "healthy" (I don't feel well).
  • -ING vs. -ED Adjectives: -ING describes the cause of a feeling (an interesting case, a tiring museum visit). -ED describes the person's emotion (I am interested in art, she felt tired).
1. Giorgia speaks English very _______ because she practises consistently.
2. The guide at the Louvre shared a _______ story about Renaissance painters.
3. Walking around Florence for five hours was exhausting, so we felt very _______ in the evening.
4. The group therapy session didn't go very _______ because Greg kept interrupting everyone.
5. Masaccio's use of linear perspective was a _______ breakthrough for 15th-century art.

3. Direction Verbs (Bring vs. Take) & Verb Patterns

Direction & Meaning Shifts:
  • Bring vs. Take: Use bring for movement towards the speaker/listener ("Bring your notes here"). Use take for movement away to another place ("Take your coat when you leave").
  • Remember / Stop + -ing vs. to + infinitive:
    Remember to lock = don't forget to do a future task. Remember locking = have a memory of a past action.
    Stop working = quit/finish the activity. Stop to work = pause another activity in order to start working.
  • Used to + base verb: Describes a past habit or state that is no longer true ("I used to play the piano").
1. When you come to our next lesson, please _______ your grammar notebook with you.
2. Don't forget to _______ your umbrella when you leave the apartment—it's pouring outside!
3. Please remember _______ the front door before you go to bed tonight.
4. At 7:00 PM, she finally stopped _______ because she had finished all her exercises.
5. When I was a child, I _______ watch medical dramas every evening with my family.

4. Quantifiers Masterclass: Much, Many, A Lot (of), Enough, Too & So

Complete Quantifier Summary:
  • Much vs. Many vs. A lot (of): Use much with uncountable nouns mainly in negatives/questions (not much time). Use many with plural countable nouns. Use a lot of in positive sentences for both; use a lot without a noun at the end of a clause ("She reads a lot").
  • All of: Refers to 100% of a specific group (all of the students).
  • Too vs. So: Too / too much / too many = an excessive, problematic amount (too fast, too much noise). So / so much / so many = strong positive or dramatic emphasis (so many paintings, thank you so much).
  • Enough: Goes before nouns (enough time) and after adjectives/adverbs (fast enough).
1. We don't have _______ time left before the session finishes, so let's do one more exercise.
2. There were _______ tourists around the painting that we couldn't take a clear photo.
3. Please don't speak _______ quickly—I want to understand every single word!
4. Masaccio achieved _______ during his short career, influencing generations of artists.
5. _______ the patients in the ward were surprised when Dr. House started diagnosing them.
6. Did you have _______ time to review all the notes from Lesson 5?

5. Narrative Tenses, Reported Speech & Modals

Structuring Stories & Reporting Statements:
  • Past Perfect (had + past participle): Used for an action completed before another past moment ("By the time the doctor arrived, the patient had fallen asleep").
  • Reported Speech: When reporting what someone said in the past, shift verbs one step back (Present Simple → Past Simple; will → would) and adjust time words (today → that day, tomorrow → the next day).
  • Prohibition vs. Advice: Use mustn't when a rule forbids something.
1. By the time we arrived at the Brancacci Chapel, it _______ dark outside.
2. "I love Renaissance art," Giorgia said. → Giorgia said that she _______ Renaissance art.
3. In the psychiatric ward, patients _______ mention taboo topics during group therapy—it's against the rules.
4. The therapist gave the patients some very helpful _______ about managing anger.
5. House realized that Dr. Nolan _______ to give the other patients what they wanted except him.

6. Video Lab: House M.D. — Diagnostic Instincts in Mayfield

📋 Context & Synopsis: Who is Dr. House & What is Happening?

Who is Dr. Gregory House? Dr. Gregory House is the brilliant, sarcastic, and deeply cynical protagonist of the hit medical drama House M.D. Known for his sharp intellect, limp (requiring a cane), and disregard for conventional rules, House normally leads a team of diagnosticians solving mysterious medical cases.

What is the situation in this clip? Following a severe mental health crisis and addiction to painkillers (Vicodin), House has checked himself into Mayfield Psychiatric Hospital as a patient rather than a doctor. In this scene, he joins his first group therapy session and recreational activities alongside eccentric fellow patients—including his energetic roommate Alvie, a claustrophobic patient named Jay, and a silent woman who only responds to music. Unable to turn off his analytical mind, House refuses to cooperate with the therapists and immediately begins observing, provoking, and "diagnosing" everyone around him—until his doctor, Dr. Nolan, challenges him to stop fighting the system and use his leadership to actually help the ward.

Video Comprehension Check

1. Why does Dr. Beasley end the group therapy session early on House's first day?
2. What reaction does House notice from the silent female patient when Alvie plays the piano?
3. What complaint does House use to rally the other patients in the recreation room?
4. How does Dr. Nolan respond to House's rebellion?
5. What does Dr. Nolan tell House he could do on the ward instead of fighting?

7. Active Writing & Sentence Transformation

Apply all your grammar skills in writing. Rewrite the sentences below and check the model answers, then write a short scene summary.

A. Reported Speech: "I am trying to help you," Dr. Beasley told House.

Dr. Beasley told House that she was trying to help him.

B. Past Perfect: First, House checked into Mayfield. Then, he joined group therapy.

By the time House joined group therapy, he had checked into Mayfield.

C. Quantifier Rewrite ('enough'): The ping-pong table wasn't complete because there weren't paddles or a net. (Rewrite using "enough equipment")

The patients didn't have enough equipment to play table tennis properly.

D. Free Writing Challenge: Write 4–5 sentences describing either your favorite scene from a medical drama (like Grey's Anatomy or House M.D.) or a visit to a museum. Try to include at least 4 of these elements:

  • ✓ -ed or -ing adjective
  • ✓ Past Perfect (had + past participle)
  • ✓ Adverb of manner (well / quickly / fast)
  • ✓ Quantifier (so many / too much / enough / a lot)
  • ✓ Bring or Take
