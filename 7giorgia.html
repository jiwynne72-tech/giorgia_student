Your Study Notes & Practice (B1+/B2)

Quick, clear rules from your notes plus extra exam-style practice to build accuracy and confidence.

1. Grammar & Verb Usage

-ing Forms for Activities

Use -ing when an action works like a noun (as a subject, after prepositions, or for hobbies and tasks).

Example: Wrapping gifts takes time. / We spent the evening preparing antipasti.

During vs. For / While

Use during + noun (when), for + length of time (how long), and while + subject & verb.

Example: We stayed inside during the storm (for two hours / while it rained).

Something vs. Anything

Use something in positive sentences (and polite offers). Use anything in negatives and general questions.

Example: I need something to eat. / I don't have anything to wear. / Did you see anything?

Don't vs. Won't

Use don't for present habits, routines, or facts. Use won't for future predictions, decisions, or refusal.

Example: I don't drink coffee late. / He won't arrive until tomorrow.

Hasn't vs. Doesn't

Use hasn't + past participle for present perfect (past linked to now). Use doesn't + base verb for present simple routines.

Example: She hasn't finished yet. / She doesn't eat meat.

Gave us (Irregular Past)

The past simple of give is gave—never gaves. Use gives only for present simple he/she/it.

Example: Our teacher gave us extra practice yesterday.

Don't Drop the Object

Many English verbs (like explain, tell, give, bring) need an object so your sentence sounds complete and natural.

Example: Can you explain that to me? / I'll bring it tomorrow.

"Would like …"

Use would like + to + verb for an action, or would like + noun to ask for something politely.

Example: I would like to check my score. / We would like two waters, please.

In summer vs. In the summer

Both are natural. In summer is common for seasons in general; in the summer often points to a specific or familiar season.

Example: It gets hot here in summer / in the summer.

2. Vocabulary, Phrasal Verbs & Word Formation

Tell off

To tell someone off means to scold them for doing something wrong or breaking a rule.

Example: The coach told him off for being late.

Go back vs. Return

Go back is casual (often go back to a place). Return is more formal (return to a place; neither uses to before home).

Example: I'm going back home now. / We'll return to school next week.

In season

In season means available at a particular time of year—like seasonal produce or limited-edition flavors (e.g., special Japanese Kit-Kats).

Example: Strawberries are in season in spring. / Japan has seasonal Kit-Kat flavors.

Suffix -ful & Explain

Add -ful (always one "l") to make adjectives like helpful, careful, useful. The noun from explain is explanation.

Example: Thanks for the helpful explanation.

Noun Modifiers & Place Names

A noun can describe another noun (glass balls). Capitalize proper names and use the with landmarks like the Empire State Building.

Example: We bought glass balls and visited the Empire State Building.

Emotions & Animation

Handy words for speaking and writing: feeling words (anxious, relieved, frustrated, delighted) and animation (showing strong or subtle emotions on screen).

Example: Animation makes it easy to show characters' emotions.

3. Coursebook & Exam Focus

Into Focus & B1+/B2 Exams

In Into Focus and B1 PET / B2 exams, examiners reward clean verb forms, natural phrasal verbs, accurate spelling/word formation, and complete sentences.

4. Practice Exercises

Exercise A: Choose the Best Option (8 Items)

  1. We stayed inside __________ the heavy storm. (during / while)
  2. I don't have __________ to wear for the party. (something / anything)
  3. The teacher __________ him off for copying. (told / explained)
  4. Yesterday, she __________ us a great exam tip. (gave / gaves)
  5. He __________ finished his homework yet. (hasn't / doesn't)
  6. We stayed in London __________ three days. (during / for)
  7. I __________ eat fast food on weekdays. (don't / won't)
  8. Would you like __________ tea or coffee? (some / any)
Show Answers (Exercise A)
  • 1. during (+ noun phrase the heavy storm)
  • 2. anything (negative sentence)
  • 3. told (tell someone off = scold)
  • 4. gave (past simple of give)
  • 5. hasn't (present perfect + yet)
  • 6. for (duration: three days)
  • 7. don't (present simple routine)
  • 8. some (polite offer)

Exercise B: Complete with the Right Form (8 Items)

  1. __________ (wrap) gifts takes longer than people think.
  2. I __________ (not / eat) breakfast before 8 a.m.
  3. What time do you __________ (go back / return) home after class?
  4. I __________ (would like) a quick __________ (explain) of this rule.
  5. Peaches are __________ (in) season right now, especially __________ (in) summer.
  6. She __________ (not / reply) to my message yet.
  7. Don't worry—I promise I __________ (not / tell) anyone.
  8. We spent hours __________ (watch) classic animation clips.
Show Answers (Exercise B)
  • 1. Wrapping (-ing activity as subject)
  • 2. don't eat (present simple negative)
  • 3. go back or return (both natural before home)
  • 4. would like / explanation (polite request + noun form)
  • 5. in / in (in season; in summer)
  • 6. hasn't replied (present perfect negative with yet)
  • 7. won't tell (future promise/decision)
  • 8. watching (spend time + -ing)

Exercise C: Spot and Fix the Mistake (8 Items)

  1. He gaves us two tickets for the Empire State Building.
  2. She doesn't have seen that animation film yet.
  3. Can you explain? (Add a natural object)
  4. That was a very helpfull note.
  5. We met during we were on holiday.
  6. I didn't buy something at the shop.
  7. He return to Milan every Friday.
  8. She won't drinks tea in the morning.
Show Answers (Exercise C)
  • 1. Change gaves to gave.
  • 2. Change doesn't have to hasn't.
  • 3. Add an object, e.g., "Can you explain it / that?"
  • 4. Change helpfull to helpful (one l).
  • 5. Change during to while (before a clause with subject + verb).
  • 6. Change something to anything (negative sentence).
  • 7. Change return to returns (third-person singular -s).
  • 8. Change won't drinks to doesn't drink (habit) or won't drink (future/refusal).
