1. Grammar & Verb Usage

-ing Forms for Activities & Gerunds

Use -ing forms (gerunds) to name activities, hobbies, or actions treated as nouns—especially after prepositions, after verbs of liking/disliking, or when talking about general activities (such as wrapping gifts or preparing antipasti).

Example: "Wrapping gifts takes time." / "We spent the evening preparing antipasti."

During (and Similar Time Expressions)

Use during before a noun or noun phrase to say when something happens over a period of time (not to measure how long it lasts—use for + duration for length of time, and while + clause with a subject and verb).

Example: "We stayed inside during the storm." (Compare: "for two hours" / "while it was raining".)

Something vs. Anything

Use something in positive statements (and in offers/requests where you expect "yes"). Use anything in negative sentences and most general questions.

Example: "I need something to eat." / "I don't have anything to wear." / "Did you find anything?"

Don't vs. Won't

Don't is the negative present simple (do not) for habits, facts, routines, or imperatives. Won't is the negative future (will not) for future predictions, decisions, or refusal.

Example: "I don't drink coffee in the evening." / "She won't be here until tomorrow."

Hasn't vs. Doesn't

Hasn't (has not) is used in the present perfect with third-person singular subjects (he, she, it) + past participle to connect past actions to now. Doesn't (does not) is used in the present simple with he, she, it + base verb for routines or general truths.

Example: "He hasn't finished his homework yet." / "He doesn't like spicy food."

Gave us vs. Gaves us

Give is an irregular verb: give – gave – given. The past simple form is gave—never add -s in the past tense ("gaves" is incorrect). In the present simple for he/she/it, the form is gives.

Example: "The teacher gave us extra practice." (Not *"gaves us"*.)

Avoiding "Object Forgetting"

In English, transitive verbs (like give, tell, bring, explain, buy) and prepositions usually require an explicit object or complement rather than leaving the sentence incomplete.

Example: Say "Can you explain it to me?" or "I'll bring the notes tomorrow," rather than dropping a needed object.

"Would like …"

Would like is a polite way to say "want." Follow it with to + infinitive for an action or a noun phrase for something you want to have/order.

Example: "I would like to book a table." / "We would like two coffees, please."

In summer vs. In the summer

Both are grammatical! In summer (zero article) is very common in general statements about seasons. In the summer (with the) is also natural and often used when referring to a specific or familiar seasonal period.

Example: "It gets very hot here in summer / in the summer."

2. Vocabulary, Phrasal Verbs & Word Formation

Tell off (Phrasal Verb)

To tell someone off means to scold or reprimand them angrily because they did something wrong or broke a rule.

Example: "The boss told him off for arriving late again."

Go back vs. Return

Go back is informal and everyday; it often takes to before a place (go back to Rome). Return is more formal or neutral and does not use to when followed directly by a place (return home / return to the office—note that home takes no preposition after go back or return).

Example: "I need to go back home." / "They will return to the city next week."

In season (e.g., Seasonal / Japanese Kit-Kat Flavors)

In season describes produce, food, or limited-edition items that are currently available at a particular time of year. Seasonal/limited editions—such as special Japanese Kit-Kat flavors—are a classic real-world example of items tied to a specific time or season.

Example: "Strawberries are in season in spring." / "Japan is famous for seasonal Kit-Kat editions."

Suffixes & Word Formation (-ful Suffix & "Explain")

The suffix -ful usually forms adjectives meaning "full of" or "having the quality of" (e.g., helpful, careful, useful). Be careful with spelling: words ending in -ful have one "l" (not two). Note also that explain does not take -ful; its noun form is explanation.

Example: "Thank you for a very helpful explanation."

Compound Nouns & Noun Modifiers (Glass balls, Building, Empire State Building)

In English, a noun can modify another noun (e.g., glass balls = balls made of glass, or office building). Proper names like the Empire State Building use capital letters and usually take the.

Example: "We hung glass balls on the tree." / "We visited the Empire State Building."

Emotions & Animation

Useful topic vocabulary for describing feelings (anxious, relieved, frustrated, delighted) and visual storytelling/media (animation, character expressions, exaggerating emotions).

Example: "Animation is great at showing subtle emotions on a character's face."

4. Practice Exercises

Exercise A: Choose the Correct Option

  1. We had to stay indoors __________ the heavy rain. (during / while)
  2. I don't have __________ to wear to the party tonight. (something / anything)
  3. The manager __________ him off for missing the deadline. (told / explained)
  4. Yesterday, our teacher __________ us a great tip for the B2 exam. (gave / gaves)
  5. She __________ finished her report yet, so wait a few minutes. (hasn't / doesn't)
Show Answers & Explanations (Exercise A)
  • 1. during — Followed by the noun phrase the heavy rain.
  • 2. anything — Used in a negative sentence (don't have).
  • 3. told — Tell someone off means to scold/reprimand.
  • 4. gave — Past simple of give is gave (never gaves).
  • 5. hasn't — Present perfect with yet + past participle (finished).

Exercise B: Complete with the Right Form

  1. __________ (wrap) presents is my favorite part of getting ready for the holidays.
  2. I __________ (not / work) on Sundays, so I'm free to meet.
  3. What time do you plan to __________ (return / go back) home tonight?
  4. I __________ (would like) a clearer __________ (explain) of this rule, please.
  5. Cherries are __________ (in) season right now, and I love eating them __________ (in) summer.
Show Answers & Explanations (Exercise B)
  • 1. Wrapping — Gerund (-ing) functioning as the subject/activity.
  • 2. don't work — Present simple negative for a regular routine.
  • 3. return (or go back) — Both work naturally before home.
  • 4. would like / explanation — Polite request + noun form of explain.
  • 5. in / in — Fixed expression in season; in summer (or in the summer) for the season.

Exercise C: Spot and Fix the Mistake

  1. He gaves us two tickets for the tour of the Empire State Building.
  2. She doesn't have visited the animation museum yet.
  3. Can you explain? (Fix by adding a natural object)
  4. That was a very helpfull answer.
Show Answers & Explanations (Exercise C)
  • 1. Change gaves to gave (irregular past tense of give).
  • 2. Change doesn't have to hasn't (present perfect for an unfinished time up to now).
  • 3. Add an object, e.g., "Can you explain that / how it works?" (avoids object forgetting).
  • 4. Change helpfull to helpful (single l in the -ful suffix).
