1. Know Yourself: Personality & Word Formation

💡 Word Formation: Nouns → Positive & Negative Adjectives

In English (and in B1/B2 exam tasks!), we often build positive and negative personality adjectives from abstract nouns using suffixes (-ous, -ly, -est, -kind, -tient, -lite, -ible, -tive) and negative prefixes (un-, dis-, in-, im-, ir-).

Prefix un- & dis-

ambition → ambitious / unambitious
friendliness → friendly / unfriendly
kindness → kind / unkind
honesty → honest / dishonest

Prefix im-, ir- & in-

patience → patient / impatient
politeness → polite / impolite
responsibility → responsible / irresponsible
sensitivity → sensitive / insensitive

3. Grammar: Past Perfect Simple & Continuous

⏰ Clear & Simple Grammar Guide

We use the Past Perfect ("the past before the past") to make it clear that an activity happened before the main events in a past story.

1. Past Perfect Simple (had + past participle)

Use it for a completed action that happened before another past action, or to focus on how many times / how much was completed.

He assumed she had got married.

2. Past Perfect Continuous (had + been + -ing)

Use it for an activity in progress before a past moment, or to emphasize duration (for, since, all night) or a visible past result.

Steve had been working in a factory.

3. State Verbs = Simple ONLY!

Never use continuous forms with state verbs (be, have, know, love, believe, understand, want), even with for or since!

It had been there for ten years.

4. Time Expressions (before / after)

You don't have to use the Past Perfect if before or after already makes the order of events 100% clear (both Past Simple and Past Perfect are fine).

They got married after they (had) met.

📖 Story Reference: "It was meant to be" (Page 26)

Amelie was studying English in the UK when she met Steve. It was love at first sight and they spent a wonderful summer together. Amelie returned to France after her course finished. They tried to keep a long-distance relationship going, but they drifted apart.

A few years later, Steve wrote to Amelie but Amelie's mother put the letter on the mantelpiece, forgot to tell Amelie, and it slipped down the back of the fireplace. Many years later, a builder was removing the fireplace when he found the letter. It had been there for ten years.

During that time, Amelie had remained single and had never forgotten the love of her life. Meanwhile, Steve had been working in a factory in his home town. He had been thinking about Amelie too, but he assumed she had got married to somebody else.

When she finally read the letter, Amelie was too nervous to call as so much time had passed. But she finally plucked up the courage and they arranged to meet. They got married seventeen years after they had first met.

💕 Relationship Phrases & Prepositions

Pay close attention to the exact prepositions in these relationship expressions:

= fancy sb (sentirsi attratti da qualcuno)

= adore sb (essere follemente innamorati di qualcuno)

🎙️ Interview Transcript Summary: Dr Ruby Niverton

Interviewer: Today we're talking to neuroscientist Dr Ruby Niverton, who studies how chemical reactions in the brain affect our emotions and behaviour. Dr Niverton, from a scientific point of view, what is romantic love?

Dr Niverton: Well, brain scans show that romantic love isn't just a gentle emotion—it actually behaves very much like an addiction! When someone falls madly in love, specific areas of the brain flood with dopamine and oxytocin, creating intense focus and craving.

Interviewer: What did you see when you put people in love inside an MRI scanner?

Dr Niverton: Interestingly, we saw that two distinct parts of the brain become highly activated at the same time—the reward centre and the area linked to motivation. Even more fascinating: when a person gets heartbroken and splits up with their partner, that love-related activity doesn't just switch off. In fact, experiments found it can actually increase at first, which explains why heartbreak feels so obsessive! Ultimately, our findings demonstrate that powerful human emotions aren't purely mysterious—they can be scientifically measured.
